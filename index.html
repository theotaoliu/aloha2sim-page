aloha2sim: A MuJoCo Twin of the Lab ALOHA-2 Workcell

A simulation of the physical cell rather than of a generic one — the same table markings, the same 31.1 mm cubes and waypoints taken from the recorded episodes — built so that RLinf can drive it for online RL fine-tuning of π0 / π0.5.

Simulated Rollout

The scene is Task 1 of the real-robot study, hidden-property pick-and-place, rebuilt in MuJoCo. Geometry is not invented: the pick point and the three target zones are the forward-kinematic fingertip positions of 54 usable recorded episodes, and the table markings were measured on the real rig and cross-checked against the overhead camera frame.

The Task

Three cubes — A, B, C — share one 31.1 mm hollow shell and one colour, and differ only in what a camera cannot see: A and B in mass at equal surface friction, A and C in friction at equal mass. One cube is present per episode and each has its own target, so the destination cannot be read off the pre-grasp image. Targets are 45 mm apart, which caps the success radius at 20 mm; an episode is 500 control steps, 10 s at 50 Hz. Since evaluation is balanced across the three cubes, a policy that always releases in the same region scores exactly 1/3 by construction — the task's collapse floor, not a level of ability.

What the Model Reproduces

The robot

mujoco_menagerie's system-identified ALOHA 2, forked into the repo only once an include could no longer be overridden, and proven field-for-field identical to the wrapped model before anything was changed.

The gripper

Command-to-finger travel fitted from 45 recorded episodes on quasi-static free-motion samples, R² 0.946 and 0.978. The real robot's second, reporting map is reproduced rather than collapsed, because a policy trained on real data sees both.

The table

Markers and cross measured against two frame rails and cross-checked against the overhead frame — which caught a 54 mm bias in the positions first extracted from the recordings.

The cameras and the cube

Poses and intrinsics from the lab's own calibration instead of menagerie's D405 defaults, wrist cameras placed on the D435i's RGB lens from CAD; the cube is a hollow shell compiled with exact inertia.

Not simulated. There is no tactile sensing in the model. The cube's identity is therefore not inferable from the observation, only from privileged state — the physical difference is real, but this is a manipulation task with a hidden variable rather than a tactile one. The mass and friction values carry the documented ordering, not measured magnitudes.
